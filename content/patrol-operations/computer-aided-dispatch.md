# Computer-Aided Dispatch Systems

Computer-aided dispatch (CAD) systems help law enforcement agencies manage calls for service and coordinate officers in the field. Dispatchers use CAD systems to create calls, assign units, record important updates and document activity associated with an incident.

## Managing Calls for Service

When a person contacts a communications center, information about the call can be entered into the CAD system. This may include the location, type of incident, caller information and details that responding officers need to know.

As additional information becomes available, dispatchers can update the call. Officers using [[mobile-data-computers|Mobile Data Computers]] may then receive those updates directly in their patrol vehicles.

## Officer and Unit Status

CAD systems can also help dispatchers track the status of officers and calls. Depending on the agency's system, dispatchers may be able to determine whether an officer is available, responding to a call, on scene or completing another activity.

This information is important during emergencies because dispatchers need to know which resources are available. CAD therefore works alongside [[communication-technology|Communication Technology]] to help coordinate officers, dispatchers and supervisors.

## Documentation and Analysis

CAD records can provide a timeline of activity associated with a call for service. Information such as dispatch times, arrival times and call updates may later assist with reports, investigations or administrative review.

Calls for service can also provide useful information for [[crime-analysis|Crime Analysis]]. Examining repeated calls at particular locations or during certain time periods may help agencies recognize recurring problems.

Computer-aided dispatch systems organize large amounts of information and help agencies coordinate their response to calls. Their effectiveness depends on accurate information, reliable technology and clear communication between dispatchers and officers.
