# Mobile Data Computers

Mobile data computers (MDCs) give patrol officers access to important information from inside their vehicles. These systems may allow officers to receive calls for service, view dispatch information, check vehicle or driver information, access maps and complete reports without returning to the police station.

## Access to Information

One of the main advantages of an MDC is the ability to receive information while an officer is in the field. Dispatchers can send call details directly to responding officers, including addresses, updates and other information related to an incident.

MDCs also work closely with [[communication-technology|Communication Technology]]. Radio communication remains important, but data transmitted through an MDC can reduce unnecessary radio traffic and provide officers with information that may be difficult to communicate verbally.

## Patrol Operations

During routine patrol, officers may use an MDC to conduct authorized database inquiries, review call history or obtain information related to a vehicle or person. Access to this information can improve situational awareness before an officer arrives at a call.

Mobile computers can also work with [[vehicle-technology|Vehicle Technology]] and mapping systems to help officers locate calls and navigate unfamiliar areas.

## Security and Officer Safety

Because MDCs may provide access to sensitive law enforcement information, officers must protect their login credentials and follow agency security policies. Information displayed on the computer should only be accessed for legitimate law enforcement purposes.

The location of the computer inside a patrol vehicle can also create a distraction. Officers must balance the need to receive information with the responsibility to operate their vehicles safely.

Mobile data computers have become an important part of modern patrol operations. When used properly, they allow officers to access information more efficiently while remaining available in the field.
