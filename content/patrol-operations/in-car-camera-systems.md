# In-Car Camera Systems

In-car camera systems are used in patrol vehicles to record traffic stops, vehicle pursuits and other law enforcement activity. These systems can provide video and audio evidence of an incident while also documenting an officer's actions during encounters with the public.

## Recording Patrol Activity

In-car cameras are typically positioned to capture activity occurring in front of a patrol vehicle. Depending on the system, recording may begin automatically when emergency equipment is activated or may be started manually by the officer.

These systems can work alongside [[body-worn-cameras|Body-Worn Cameras and Officer Safety]]. While a body-worn camera records from the officer's position, an in-car camera provides another perspective that may capture events differently.

## Evidence and Documentation

Recordings from in-car cameras can become [[digital-evidence|Digital Evidence]]. Video may document driving behavior before a traffic stop, statements made during an encounter or events that occur during a vehicle pursuit.

Because these recordings may later be used in criminal or administrative proceedings, agencies need procedures for uploading, storing and retaining the files. Officers should also document relevant recordings in their reports according to agency policy.

## Limitations

In-car cameras do not capture everything an officer sees. The camera generally records from a fixed position, and activity may occur outside its field of view. Lighting, weather, distance and obstructions can also affect the quality of a recording.

A recording should therefore be considered alongside the officer's observations, reports and other available evidence.

In-car cameras provide an additional source of documentation for patrol activity. When combined with [[vehicle-technology|Vehicle Technology]] and body-worn cameras, they can provide useful evidence while helping agencies maintain accurate records of police encounters.
