# Community Partnerships and Problem Solving

Community policing depends on relationships between law enforcement agencies and the people they serve. Police departments cannot address every community problem through enforcement alone. Partnerships with residents, businesses, schools and other local organizations can help agencies identify concerns and develop solutions before those concerns become larger problems.

## Identifying Community Problems

Officers regularly encounter issues that may not have an immediate law enforcement solution. Examples can include repeated disturbances, traffic complaints, abandoned properties or concerns about activity in a particular area. Looking at repeated calls and community complaints can help officers determine whether separate incidents are actually part of a larger problem.

Technology can support this process. Information from [[community-engagement|Community Engagement Technology]] can provide another way for residents to communicate concerns, while [[crime-analysis|Crime Analysis]] can help identify patterns in calls for service and reported offenses.

## Developing Partnerships

Different problems may require different community partners. Schools may work with law enforcement on campus safety concerns, while businesses may assist with crime-prevention efforts in commercial areas. Neighborhood organizations can also provide information about problems that officers may not observe during routine patrol.

These partnerships allow police departments to combine law enforcement resources with the knowledge and resources already available within the community.

## Technology as a Supporting Tool

Technology can improve communication and provide officers with additional information, but it cannot create a successful community partnership by itself. Data must be interpreted in context, and residents need opportunities to communicate directly with the officers serving their neighborhoods.

Effective problem solving combines technology, officer observations and community involvement. When these resources are used together, agencies can address recurring problems while maintaining the relationships that are central to community policing.
