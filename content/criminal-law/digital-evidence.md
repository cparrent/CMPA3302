# Digital Evidence

Digital evidence has become increasingly common in criminal investigations. Cell phones, computers, surveillance systems, social media accounts and other electronic devices can contain information relevant to a criminal case. Law enforcement officers must understand how digital evidence differs from traditional physical evidence and how constitutional protections apply to its collection.

## Collection and Legal Authority

The Fourth Amendment continues to apply when officers seek digital evidence. Depending on the circumstances, investigators may need a search warrant, consent or another recognized legal justification before searching a device or obtaining certain electronic records.

This makes digital evidence closely related to [[search-and-seizure|Search and Seizure]]. Technology may provide investigators with valuable information, but access to that information must still comply with applicable law.

## Preservation and Integrity

Digital evidence must be preserved in a way that protects its integrity. Investigators should document how evidence was obtained, handled and transferred. Maintaining this record helps establish that the evidence presented later is the same information that was originally collected.

Some digital information can also be changed or deleted quickly. Proper preservation procedures are therefore important when investigators encounter electronic devices or online information.

Digital evidence collected from devices may later require examination using [[digital-forensics|Digital Forensics]] techniques. Other technologies, including [[body-worn-cameras|Body-Worn Cameras and Officer Safety]], can create digital evidence during routine police activity.

## Social Media and Online Information

Social media can also become a source of evidence. Public posts, photographs, videos and messages may provide investigative leads or information related to an offense. This connects digital evidence with [[social-media-and-public-information|Social Media and Public Information]].

As technology becomes more integrated into everyday life, digital evidence will continue to play an important role in criminal investigations. Proper legal authority, preservation and documentation are necessary to ensure that this information can be used effectively and responsibly.
