# Radar and Lidar

Radar and lidar are technologies used by law enforcement officers to measure vehicle speed and support traffic enforcement. Although both systems are used for similar purposes, they measure speed differently and have different operational characteristics.

## Radar

Radar uses radio waves to determine the speed of a moving vehicle. Depending on the equipment and mode being used, officers may operate radar while stationary or while the patrol vehicle is moving.

An important part of speed enforcement is the officer's own observation. Radar provides a measurement that can support what the officer observes, but officers must still properly operate the equipment and follow their training and agency procedures.

## Lidar

Lidar uses laser pulses rather than radio waves to measure speed. The officer aims the device at a specific vehicle, and the system calculates speed based on changes in distance over a very short period of time.

Because the beam is narrowly focused, lidar can help an officer identify a particular vehicle in heavier traffic. Like radar, however, its reliability depends on proper operation and the condition of the equipment.

## Documentation and Enforcement

Officers using either technology should follow applicable training requirements, agency policy and procedures for checking the equipment. Weather, traffic conditions, location and other environmental factors may also affect how speed enforcement is conducted.

Speed information can contribute to broader traffic-safety efforts. Areas with repeated speeding complaints or crashes may receive additional enforcement attention based on community concerns and agency data.

Radar and lidar demonstrate how technology can support an officer's observations rather than replace them. When properly operated, these systems provide officers with an additional tool for enforcing traffic laws and addressing unsafe driving behavior.
