# Electronic Citations

Electronic citation systems allow officers to create traffic citations using computers, tablets or handheld devices instead of completing paper citations by hand. These systems can improve efficiency by allowing information to be entered electronically and transferred to court or records systems.

## Creating Citations

During a traffic stop, an officer may use an electronic citation system to enter information about the driver, vehicle, location and alleged violation. Some systems can import information from a driver's license or other law enforcement databases, reducing the amount of information that must be entered manually.

Electronic citation systems may work alongside [[mobile-data-computers|Mobile Data Computers]] and other equipment already installed in patrol vehicles. This allows officers to complete more of the traffic-stop documentation without returning to the station.

## Accuracy and Efficiency

Electronic systems can improve readability and reduce some errors associated with handwritten citations. Required fields may also help ensure that important information is entered before the citation is completed.

Technology does not eliminate the officer's responsibility to verify the information. Officers should review names, addresses, vehicle information, statutes and other details before issuing a citation.

## Records and Security

Electronic citations become part of an agency's digital records. Because these records may contain personal information, access should be limited to authorized users and protected according to agency policy.

Equipment failures or connection problems can also occur. Officers should understand their agency's procedures for situations in which the electronic citation system is unavailable.

Electronic citations demonstrate how technology can simplify routine law enforcement tasks. When properly implemented, these systems can reduce paperwork, improve accuracy and allow officers to spend less time completing administrative tasks during traffic enforcement.
