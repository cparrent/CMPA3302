# Digital Forensics

Digital forensics is the process of identifying, preserving, examining and interpreting information stored on electronic devices. Modern criminal investigations may involve cell phones, computers, tablets, storage devices and other digital systems. These devices can contain communications, photographs, location information and other data that may become relevant to an investigation.

## Preserving Digital Evidence

One of the first concerns when handling electronic evidence is preservation. Digital information can be altered, overwritten or deleted, so investigators must take steps to protect the original evidence. The actions taken when a device is collected should also be documented so that its handling can later be explained.

Digital forensics is closely connected to [[digital-evidence|Digital Evidence]]. Digital evidence describes the information that may be relevant to a case, while digital forensics provides methods for examining and interpreting that information.

## Examination and Analysis

Forensic examination can involve recovering files, reviewing communications, examining metadata or identifying information that has been deleted. Depending on the device and investigation, specialized software and trained personnel may be required.

Investigators must also consider the legal authority that permits an examination. The possession of a device does not automatically authorize officers to search all of its contents. Issues involving warrants and privacy connect digital forensics with [[search-and-seizure|Search and Seizure]].

## Role in Modern Investigations

Digital information can help investigators establish timelines, identify relationships between people and locate additional evidence. However, digital evidence should be evaluated alongside other investigative information rather than automatically treated as conclusive.

As electronic devices become more integrated into everyday life, digital forensics will continue to be an important investigative resource. Proper preservation, legal authority and careful analysis allow investigators to use digital information while protecting the integrity of the investigation.
