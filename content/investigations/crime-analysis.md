# Crime Analysis

Crime analysis uses information from police reports, calls for service and other law enforcement records to identify patterns and trends. Instead of viewing every incident individually, analysts and officers can examine groups of incidents to determine whether they share locations, times, methods or other characteristics.

## Identifying Patterns

One purpose of crime analysis is to identify patterns that may not be obvious during individual calls for service. For example, several thefts occurring in the same area during similar hours may indicate a pattern that deserves additional attention.

Information received through [[community-partnerships|Community Partnerships and Problem Solving]] can also provide context for these patterns. Residents and businesses may identify recurring concerns that are not immediately apparent from offense reports alone.

## Supporting Patrol and Investigations

Crime analysis can help agencies determine where and when certain problems are occurring. This information may assist supervisors when allocating patrol resources or help investigators identify similarities between cases.

Technology can make large amounts of information easier to organize and compare. Computer systems can sort incidents by location, offense type, date and other characteristics. However, the results still require interpretation by analysts and officers who understand the local environment.

Crime analysis can also work alongside technologies such as [[automated-license-plate-readers|Automated License Plate Readers]] when investigators are attempting to identify vehicles connected with criminal activity.

## Limitations and Responsible Use

Crime data does not provide a complete picture of everything occurring within a community. Some offenses are never reported, and increased police activity in one area may generate more recorded incidents than in another area.

For that reason, crime analysis should support rather than replace officer judgment and community knowledge. When used responsibly, it can help law enforcement agencies recognize patterns, allocate resources and develop more informed responses to recurring problems.
