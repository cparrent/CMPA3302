# Interview and Interrogation Technology

Interviews are an important part of criminal investigations. Investigators may interview victims, witnesses and suspects to obtain information about an incident. Technology can assist this process by creating accurate recordings and helping investigators preserve statements for later review.

## Recording Interviews

Audio and video recording systems allow investigators to document what was said during an interview. A recording can provide a more complete record than written notes alone and may allow investigators, prosecutors and defense attorneys to review the actual conversation later.

Recordings also become a form of [[digital-evidence|Digital Evidence]]. Because of this, agencies must properly store and preserve interview files so that their authenticity and integrity can be established.

## Investigative Value

Recorded interviews allow investigators to review statements and compare them with other evidence. Details that did not initially appear important may become relevant as an investigation develops. Recordings can also help investigators compare statements from multiple people and identify inconsistencies that require additional investigation.

Interview information may be considered alongside evidence examined through [[digital-forensics|Digital Forensics]], physical evidence, surveillance footage and other investigative resources.

## Legal and Practical Considerations

Technology does not replace the legal requirements associated with interviewing suspects. Investigators must still follow applicable constitutional requirements, state law and agency policy. The circumstances surrounding an interview, including whether a person is in custody, can affect which legal protections apply.

Recording equipment also has practical limitations. Poor audio quality, equipment failure or an obstructed camera can reduce the usefulness of a recording. Investigators should verify that equipment is functioning properly and continue documenting important information through appropriate reports.

Technology can improve the documentation of interviews, but effective interviewing still depends on preparation, communication skills and sound investigative practices.
