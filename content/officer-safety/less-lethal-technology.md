# Less-Lethal Technology

Law enforcement agencies may provide officers with less-lethal tools intended to offer additional options when responding to certain situations. These tools exist within a larger use-of-force framework and do not eliminate the need for training, communication, judgment or adherence to agency policy and applicable law.

## Technology and Decision-Making

The value of less-lethal technology is not simply the existence of another piece of equipment. Officers must understand the capabilities and limitations of the equipment they are authorized to use. Circumstances can change rapidly, meaning technology cannot make decisions for the officer.

Communication can remain important during these incidents. Effective [[communication-technology|communication technology]] allows officers to coordinate with other responding personnel and communicate changing conditions.

## Documentation and Review

Technology can also contribute to how incidents are documented afterward. [[body-worn-cameras|Body-worn cameras]] may provide video and audio information that can be considered alongside written reports, witness accounts and other available evidence.

### Technology Has Limits

No piece of equipment can guarantee a particular result. Environmental conditions, distance, equipment performance and the circumstances of an encounter can affect outcomes. For this reason, training and decision-making remain essential. 

Less-lethal technology is best understood as one component of a larger officer-safety system. [[vehicle-technology|Patrol vehicle technology]], communication systems, cameras and other resources all provide different forms of information or capability. Their usefulness ultimately depends on officers understanding when and how those resources fit within their responsibilities.
