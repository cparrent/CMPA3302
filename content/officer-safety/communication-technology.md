# Communication Technology

Communication is one of the most important components of officer safety. Police officers frequently operate away from supervisors and other officers, making reliable communication with dispatchers and responding units essential. Modern law enforcement agencies use several technologies to exchange information and coordinate responses.
## Common Communication Tools

- Portable police radios 
- Computer-aided dispatch systems 
- Mobile data terminals 
- Emergency alert buttons
- Interoperable radio systems

Portable radios allow officers to communicate with dispatchers and other units while away from their patrol vehicles. Computer-aided dispatch systems provide additional information about calls for service, locations and responding units. Mobile computers can provide officers with information without requiring every detail to be transmitted over the radio.

## Communication and Officer Safety

Communication becomes especially important during emergencies. An officer requesting assistance needs other officers and dispatchers to understand what is happening and where assistance is needed. This creates a natural relationship between communication systems and [[location-tracking|location-tracking technology]]. Communication equipment is also closely connected with [[vehicle-technology|patrol vehicle technology]]. Radios, mobile computers, navigation systems and dispatch information often work together inside the patrol vehicle. 

### Technology Does Not Replace Communication Skills

Technology can improve the speed and availability of information, but it cannot replace clear communication. Officers must still provide useful information and understand agency procedures. Technology is most effective when it supports, rather than replaces, good communication practices.
