# Patrol Vehicle Technology 

The modern patrol vehicle functions as more than transportation. It can serve as a mobile workspace containing communication, navigation, emergency and information systems. These technologies allow officers to receive information and perform certain tasks while working throughout their assigned areas.
## Technology Inside the Patrol Vehicle

A patrol vehicle may contain several forms of technology, including mobile computers, radios, emergency equipment, cameras and navigation systems. Although these systems perform different functions, they often work together to provide officers with information.
### Mobile Data Computers

Mobile data computers can provide access to computer-aided dispatch information and other authorized resources. Receiving information electronically can supplement radio communication and allow officers to review details associated with calls for service. 

This makes vehicle systems closely related to [[communication-technology|communication technology]]. Information sent by dispatch can be received through both radio and computer systems depending on the agency and situation.

### Cameras and Location Systems

Some patrol vehicles are equipped with camera systems that document events occurring around the vehicle. These systems can complement [[body-worn-cameras|body-worn cameras]] by providing another perspective of an incident. 

Navigation and GPS equipment can similarly work with [[location-tracking|location-tracking technology]]. These systems can help officers navigate unfamiliar locations while also providing useful location information.

## Technology as a Tool

Having more technology inside a patrol vehicle does not automatically make an officer safer. Officers must understand the equipment and avoid allowing screens or other devices to become unnecessary distractions. When properly integrated into police operations, however, vehicle technology can improve access to information, communication and situational awareness.
